Option 4 · Reconstruction

Photogrammetry / NeRF Mesh

COLMAP + Instant-NGP / Meshroom — the classic textured-mesh route.

Mesh · R2 useful
In one line: the classic approach — reconstruct a textured 3D mesh (solid surfaces) using photogrammetry or NeRF. Great for geometry and navigation, but less photorealistic than splats for shiny / view-dependent surfaces.

Outputs a watertight textured mesh (not a splat). Good solid geometry; heavier and less sparkly than 3DGS.

How it works, step by step

  • Record a phone video of the room.
  • COLMAP recovers camera poses and a sparse point cloud.
  • Instant-NGP (NeRF) or Meshroom/OpenMVS (photogrammetry) densifies this into a surface.
  • Export a textured mesh (.obj / .glb).
  • Show it in a simple three.js mesh viewer in the browser.

Trade-offs

✓ Strengths

  • Solid watertight geometry — ideal for navigation / collision
  • Mature, well-documented tools
  • Mesh + texture is lightweight to render
  • Directly reusable for Round 2 pathfinding

✗ Weaknesses

  • Less photorealistic than 3DGS (thin / shiny stuff breaks)
  • Instant-NGP needs a CUDA build — heavy on a 4 GB GPU + SAC
  • COLMAP slow & can fail on poor video
  • More pipeline steps to maintain
Tools: COLMAP, Instant-NGP, MeshroomInput: phone videoOutput: textured meshYour compute: GPU (Colab better)Cost: free
What we actually did
Reviewed the From-video-to-3D-object repo (COLMAP → Instant-NGP → MeshLab). Decided it's superseded by Gaussian splatting for Round 1's photorealism score, and heavy to build locally.
