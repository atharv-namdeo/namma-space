On-device Phone Splatting

Scaniverse — the phone captures and trains the splat itself.

Backup
In one line: the Scaniverse app captures and trains the splat entirely on your phone — no internet, no PC — then you export it. Fast when it works, but on-device training is fragile.

Everything up to export runs on the phone itself (pink). Great when it converges; brittle when the scene is hard.

How it works, step by step

  • Open Scaniverse → new scan → choose Splat / Gaussian Splatting mode.
  • Walk a slow overlapping loop, 1–2 m from surfaces, covering walls, corners, floor and objects at several heights (30–60s).
  • The phone processes & trains the splat (a few minutes). Wait until it looks like a real room inside the app.
  • Export → Gaussian Splat → .ply (or .spz).
  • SuperSplat to tidy, then our web viewer to host & roam.

Trade-offs

✓ Strengths

  • Completely free & offline — no account
  • Very fast when the scene is good
  • Photoreal Gaussian splat output
  • Private — nothing leaves the phone

✗ Weaknesses

  • On-device training fails on hard scenes
  • Blank walls / glass / low light → empty splat
  • Easy to export before training finishes
  • Limited by the phone's GPU
Tool: ScaniverseInput: live captureOutput: .ply / .spzYour compute: the phoneCost: free
What we actually did
We tried 3 times. Scan #1 exported a mesh (wrong type). Scans #2 & #3 were correct splat format but empty: 514,690 of 524,940 points sat on the origin with the rest on a seed sphere — i.e. training never converged (likely a feature-poor scene or export before processing finished).
Verdict for the competition
Keep as a fast backup. Can still work with a cluttered, well-lit, textured scene fully processed before export — but after 3 failures, don't rely on it as plan A.
